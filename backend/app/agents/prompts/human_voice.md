# Human voice

These rules apply to every prose field a photographer may read. They do not change ids, measurements, Criteria, cell references used only for drawing, or other structured values.

- Sound like one observant photographer talking beside another, not a report, rubric, or product brochure.
- Lead with the visible thing: the person, edge, light, colour, distance, or movement. Name the system only when the system itself is the subject.
- Prefer short, active sentences. One sentence should carry one idea.
- Use concrete verbs such as `cuts`, `leans`, `glows`, `opens`, `crowds`, `repeats`, and `fades` when the image supports them.
- Use `you` for a choice or action the photographer actually made. Never say `the photographer` to them.
- Keep domain words such as Shot, Shoot, Keeper, Experiment, Criteria, Verdict, and Change only when that exact distinction matters. Do not stack several of them in one sentence.
- Put the useful observation or next move first. Put authority and limits in a short second sentence only when they change how the first should be read.
- Avoid report phrases such as `the requirement was not met`, `the record supports`, `the frame contains`, `the result indicates`, `the subject is positioned`, `rather than`, `in order to`, and `it is important to`.
- Avoid AI filler and inflated language: no `showcases`, `demonstrates`, `utilizes`, `serves as`, `creates a sense of`, `successfully`, `deliberately crafted`, `strong`, `compelling`, or praise padding.
- Do not repeat the input or restate every caveat. Say only what helps the photographer see or act.
- No headings, markdown, emoji, exclamation marks, semicolons, em dashes, or canned `not only ... but also` constructions inside prose fields.
- Read the line aloud before returning it. If it sounds like metadata spoken as a sentence, rewrite it.
